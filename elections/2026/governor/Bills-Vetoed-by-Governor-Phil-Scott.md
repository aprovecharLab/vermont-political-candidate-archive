# Bills Vetoed by Governor Phil Scott (2017–2026)

Here is a comprehensive compilation of bills vetoed by Governor Phil Scott during his tenure, organized by term and year. Phil Scott took office as Vermont's 82nd Governor in January 2017 and is currently serving his fifth term. This data is drawn from the Vermont Legislature's official "Bills Vetoed by the Governor" pages and the Governor's office press releases.

## Summary

| Term | Biennium | Bills Vetoed |
|---|---|---|
| 1st Term | 2017–2018 | 15 unique bills (18 entries, incl. re-vetoes of S.22 and S.103) |
| 2nd Term | 2019–2020 | 6 |
| 3rd Term | 2021–2022 | ~15 (14 identified) |
| 4th Term | 2023–2024 | 17 |
| 5th Term | 2025–2026 | 13 |
| **Total** | | **~66 unique vetoes (as of September 2026)** |

Governor Scott broke Vermont's all-time veto record in June 2021 (surpassing Howard Dean's 21 vetoes) and has continued to use the veto extensively since. His veto reasons cluster around several recurring themes: **affordability and cost concerns** (property taxes, energy costs, payroll taxes), **separation of powers** (objections to the Legislature mandating budget appropriations), **housing and land use** (Act 250 expansions), and **environmental/energy policy** (renewable energy standards, climate mandates, pesticide bans). The Legislature's Democratic supermajority overrode many of his vetoes during the 2023–2024 biennium — six overrides occurred in a single, record-setting day in June 2024.

### Notes on Gaps

For several bills (particularly in 2018 and 2022), Scott's specific veto reasoning was not recoverable from the available sources. The Governor's office maintains veto letters for each bill on its website, which would contain the full rationale. One bill each from the 2021–2022 biennium could not be positively identified from the search results but is recorded on the legislature's official veto tracker; all 13 entries from the 2025–2026 biennium have now been identified and confirmed against that tracker.

## Addendum: Disclaimer on Data Uncertainty

*Note: The status or details of certain bills in this compilation remain uncertain and should be independently verified before being relied upon. Specifically:*

- ***H.157*** *and* ***H.361*** *(2022)* — *Descriptions were not recoverable from available sources, and the one unidentified bill from the 2021–2022 biennium in the legislature's veto tracker is not represented here at all.*
- ***Entries with em-dashes in the "Governor's Stated Reason" column*** *(e.g., S.197, S.105, S.40, H.196 in 2018; several 2022 and 2025–2026 bills)* — *The governor's actual veto letters for these bills were not retrieved, so their stated reasons are unknown rather than nonexistent.*
- ***H.289*** *(2024)* — *The cost estimates quoted ($150M–$450M by 2035, $4.50–$13.50/month per household) reflect contested figures from that session's political debate and were not independently audited.*
- ***Some 2018 and 2023 veto dates*** — *Exact dates (e.g., H.386, H.509 in 2023) come from secondary aggregations rather than the legislature's original tracker, which was not retrievable for those sessions.*
- ***Total veto counts*** — *The "~66 unique vetoes" figure is an approximation given duplicate entries (re-vetoes of S.22 and S.103) and the unidentified bills noted above.*

Readers are encouraged to consult the Vermont Legislature's official ["Bills Vetoed by the Governor"](https://legislature.vermont.gov/bill/vetoed/2026) page and the Governor's veto letters before citing any entry here as fact.
